# Traffic Manager gameplay

1. **Read the demand.** Each scenario supplies fixed street and property connections. Hold a connection to see its scheduled destinations and trip counts, even on a cleared map.
2. **Lay out the network.** Choose an illustrated lane preset and drag between highlighted nodes to draw a straight segment. Use **Add node** on open ground or tap a road to split it. **Clear map** keeps the required connections and landscape. Undo and redo apply to individual edits.
3. **Design around the site.** The school, houses, fields, and trees occupy real space. Road width and curvature count when checking whether a road fits. Crossing roads automatically split at a shared junction.
4. **Refine the geometry.** Select a segment to change lanes or turn on its two curve handles. Select and drag a movable node to reshape its connected roads. Drawing and moving share grid, angle, and road snaps. Make a slip lane by drawing a one-way segment between two junctions and then curving it.
5. **Set the traffic rules.** Select a junction to choose an automatically timed traffic light, an all-way stop, or an unsignalized intersection. At an unsignalized intersection, select an incoming approach to give it right of way, require a yield, or add a stop sign.
6. **Run and revise.** Compare the design with the original using the same trip schedule. Disconnected trips count as unserved. Use queues, delay, completions, and collisions to decide what to change.

School Pickup includes north, south, west, and east street connections, school access, and the pickup loop. Each location uses one connection for incoming and outgoing demand. Existing corridor saves open with their original controls. The menu offers **Try new Road Builder** to load the editable starter layout as a draft.
